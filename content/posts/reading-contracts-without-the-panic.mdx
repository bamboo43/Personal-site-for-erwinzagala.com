Contracts feel intimidating because they look like they were written to win an argument, not to help two people finish a deal. They don't have to stay that way.

This is **sample content** for the Erwin Zagala site draft — not formal legal advice.

## Start with the story, not the fine print

Before you hunt for "indemnify" and "force majeure," answer three questions in plain language:

1. **Who** is promising what to whom?
2. **When** do those promises start and end?
3. **What happens** if someone misses a deadline or walks away?

If you can't summarize those answers in a few sentences, the document is still foggy — and fog is where disputes hide.

## A short PH-law–minded checklist

- **Parties & capacity.** Are the names, business styles, and signatories correct?
- **Consideration & scope.** What exactly is being paid for or delivered?
- **Payment & timeline.** Dates, milestones, and what "done" means.
- **Liability & remedies.** Caps, warranties, and how problems get fixed.
- **Governing law & venue.** Especially when parties sit in different cities or countries.
- **Signatures & authority.** Wet ink, e-signature, board approvals — match the deal.

## Make It EZ: one habit that saves hours

Read the contract once for **structure**, once for **money**, and once for **exits**. Three passes beat one anxious skim every time.

When something still feels unclear, that's a signal — not a personal failing. Clear counsel exists so you don't have to decode everything alone.
